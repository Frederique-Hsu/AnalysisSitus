CNC turning features

Surface finishing

The recognition process begins by finding all rotational symmetries in the part. All faces "vote" for a candidate turning axis, and the axis with the highest number of votes is chosen. Faces that voted for the winning axis are grouped according to their geometric type. Other faces are tagged as unrecognized and bypassed for future milling recognition.

The following image illustrates a complex turned+milled part with the "coating" that corresponds to all three possible recognition outcomes: lathed ("cold" and "hot" colors), milled (shades of green), and unrecognized (gray).

The detected milled and unrecognized features are all spanned by the constructed maximal turned state (MTS) of geometry:

This maximal turned state is used to decompose the entire "delta volume" of the turned part onto the elementary negative volume cells (which we also call "negative cells" for brevity).

Negative volumes

The following types of negative cells are extracted from the stock bar and the constructed MTS:

  • Steps: nvStep
  • Grooves: nvGroove
  • Central holes: nvHole

The reported negative volumes should be matched with the solid geometry of the maximal turned state (MTS) and not with the original part. Let's take a part illustrated in the following image as an example.

The face n.34 is reported as a turnFace type, i.e., it is a planar feature perpendicular to the main turning axis.

The turnFaces array does not specify any further properties of a feature. Its purpose is to group faces of a specific type together. Then, because a part contains milling features, there is also an MTS solid being constructed. Because MTS is a different part, there is no matching between the face IDs in the initial part and its MTS.

Introducing such matching, i.e., the history of modification between the original part and its corresponding MTS is left for the future improvements in Analysis Situs SDK.
