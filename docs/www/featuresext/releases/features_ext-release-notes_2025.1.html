Details

This section provides more details on the new features and important bug fixes in the Extensions SDK.

SMRU, DXF

Bent pipes

In the new release of SMRU, we finalized the first version of bent pipes recognition. The following table summarizes the new part types added to the recognition framework. For all types, common features are recognized, including sheet faces, thickness faces and cutouts. The global geometric properties, such as material thickness and pipe length are also extracted.

BENT_TUBE_ROUND

Bent round tubes are thin profiles swept along a spatial path. They are normally composed of straight segments connected with round folds. For this type of geometry a medial axis path is automatically extracted.

BENT_TUBE_RECTANGULAR

Bent rectangular tubes resemble normal rectangular tubes: they have a four-sided profile and contain rounded fillet-like corners. For this type of geometry a medial axis path is automatically extracted.

BENT_WIRE

Bent wires are non-hollow objects.

WIRE

Wires are plain cylindrical non-hollow objects.

Because this mode of recognition is quite specific, it is not activated by default. You have to turn on the corresponding option in the recognizer. If activated, the recognizer operates in the normal mode and then tries to recognize a part as a bent pipe if the default recognition attempts were unsuccessful.

Contacting solids

For sheet metal structures, it is often required to propose welding as a final process. The prototype approach for identification of weldings was put together in the end of 2022 for a prospect client who wanted to implement automatic identification of weld pathways like in Verbotics Weld. Back then, we conducted a thorough R&D project and designed two algorithms that form the basis of the entire approach:

  • Fast distance measurement for detecting close solids.
  • Imprinting operator for solid intersection.

We devoted a technical article to explain different approaches to welding identification: "To automatic identification of welding joints in CAD assemblies." This algorithm is now available as an option in SMRU.

Revamped DXF with layers

The Extensions SDK started off with an open-sourced DXF translator that has been used for quite some years. We shortly realized that this translator has critical limitations, e.g., it does not allow to configure linetypes or layers easily. An alternative option was to use a commercial DXF translator shipped by a 3-rd party vendor and we devoted a big technical article on this approach back in 2022: "OpenCascade's XDE for DXF drawing generator." Our approach was acknowledged by the vendor and they promoted it on their blog as well.

Quite soon it became clear that we cannot go on with the 3-rd party option because it required a separate license and limited our flexibility for customization quite a bit. Therefore, we started yet another R&D project to revamp the open-sourced solution we used for the sake of making it more functionally rich. The new DXF translator has capacity of working with separate layers. For now, this ability is used to report bend lines.

In the future versions of SMRU, we are planning to employ layers further and report more features in the flat pattern drawings, e.g., center marks for holes.

Properties of drilled holes

The drilled holes contained in a sheet metal part are sometimes used for mounting additional accessoires, e.g., studs, bolts, brackets and whatnot. To have anchor positions for such extra mounting, we added hole center points and axes to the outcome of SMRU.

The reported axes are consistent with respect to one or another sheet metal side, so you may always flip the mounting direction globally for your part.

Cutting contour segments

SMRU reports cutting length for all contours you have in the model: the outer perimeter, and cutouts are included. It may happen that single cutting length is not enough and you need more factorized information about the cutting contours to assess machining time. For that, SMRU provides an option called -segments that decomposes each contour into its elementary primitives, such as straight line segments and arcs.

In the new release, the algorithm of segments extraction has been redesigned to provide stable output with consistent information about convexity of connection points.
